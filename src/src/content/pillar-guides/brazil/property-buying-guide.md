## Understanding the Brazil Property Market

The property market in Brazil offers diverse opportunities across its key markets. São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Brasília, Belo Horizonte, and Curitiba represent the main buying hotspots, with prices and demand varying significantly between regions.

The Central Bank of Brazil sets the benchmark interest rate, which influences mortgage pricing across all lenders. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=br) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

## Property Prices and Key Markets

Typical prices in Brazil range from R$200,000 for apartments in mid-tier cities to R$1,000,000+ in premium São Paulo and Rio neighbourhoods.

Brazil uses a system called Minha Casa Minha Vida (My House My Life), a massive government housing programme that provides subsidised financing for low and middle-income families. The programme offers below-market interest rates through Caixa Econômica Federal, the state-owned bank that dominates residential lending

## Setting Your Budget

Beyond the purchase price, you need to budget for transaction costs that typically add 4-6% including ITBI, registry fees, notary fees, and broker commission to the total. Your deposit requirement is 20-30%, though this varies by lender and your buyer profile.

ITBI (Imposto de Transmissão de Bens Imóveis) ranges from 2-3% depending on the municipality

Read our [buying costs guide](/brazil/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a detailed breakdown of all purchase-related expenses.

## Getting Finance Approved

Mortgage terms in Brazil extend up to 35 years, with 20-30 year terms most common. Lenders typically offer up to Typically 70-80% through conventional banks, with Caixa offering up to 80% for FGTS-eligible buyers loan-to-value, meaning you need at least the remainder as a deposit.

Compare rates across major lenders including Caixa Econômica Federal (dominant market share), Banco do Brasil, Itaú Unibanco, Bradesco, and Santander Brasil. Our [mortgage types guide](/brazil/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) explains the differences between available loan products.

## Finding and Securing Property

Working with a qualified real estate broker (corretor de imóveis) is the standard approach in Brazil. Research local areas thoroughly, attend viewings, and consider factors like transport links, amenities, and future development plans.

## Legal Process and Due Diligence

Property transfers in Brazil require a qualified property lawyer (advogado imobiliário) to handle the legal process. Property transfers require registration at the Cartório de Registro de Imóveis (Real Estate Registry Office). The buyer must obtain a matrícula (property certificate) confirming ownership history and any encumbrances. The escritura pública (public deed) is executed at a Tabelionato de Notas (notary office).

Professional property inspections are not standard practice in Brazil. Buyers should independently verify structural condition, check for irregular constructions (obras irregulares), and confirm all municipal permits are in order

See our [property inspection checklist](/brazil/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) for what to check before committing.

## Government Support for Buyers

Minha Casa Minha Vida provides subsidised interest rates as low as 4-5% (compared to market rates of 9-12%) for qualifying families. Income limits and property value caps apply by region. FGTS (Workers' Severance Fund) can be used toward the deposit or to reduce monthly payments
