## How Mortgages Work in Malaysia

Bank Negara Malaysia sets the Overnight Policy Rate (OPR), which directly influences the Base Rate (BR) — the primary benchmark for all Malaysian home loans since 2015. When BNM changes the OPR, banks adjust their Base Rate accordingly, and all floating-rate home loans move in tandem. The effective lending rate is BR plus a spread set by each bank based on the borrower's risk profile

## Types of Mortgages Available

Malaysian home loans are predominantly variable rate, linked to the Base Rate (BR). Products include term loans (standard repayment mortgage), flexi loans (an overdraft facility secured against property, allowing excess payments to reduce interest daily and be redrawn — very popular in Malaysia), and semi-flexi loans (allowing prepayments to reduce interest but without redraw). Fixed-rate products are uncommon. Islamic home financing (available from all banks) uses concepts like Bai Bithaman Ajil (BBA) or Musharakah Mutanaqisah (MM) — these are Shariah-compliant alternatives that are economically similar to conventional loans

## How Much Can You Borrow?

Banks use the DSR (Debt Service Ratio) — total monthly debt commitments divided by net income should not exceed 60-70% (varies by bank and income level). Higher-income borrowers may qualify for a higher DSR. Credit scoring through CCRIS (Central Credit Reference Information System) and CTOS is critical — any late payment records in the past 12 months can significantly impact approval

The maximum loan-to-value in Malaysia is Up to 90% for first and second residential properties. Third and subsequent properties are capped at 70% LTV (Bank Negara guideline to curb speculation). The MyFirst Home Scheme allows 100% financing for first-time buyers purchasing properties up to RM500,000. Loan terms extend up to Maximum 35 years, with the borrower's age plus tenure not exceeding 70. Terms of 30-35 years are common for first-time buyers. Shorter terms significantly reduce total interest cost.

## The Application Process

Check your CCRIS and CTOS reports before applying. Provide MyKad (IC), latest 3-6 months payslips, latest EPF statement, EA form or tax returns (for salaried), or 2-3 years audited accounts (self-employed), bank statements (6 months), and the Sale and Purchase Agreement (SPA). The bank conducts a property valuation. Letter of Offer is typically issued within 1-2 weeks of complete documentation. Loan documentation is executed through a lawyer

## Costs and Fees

Stamp duty on the loan agreement is 0.5% of the loan amount. Legal fees for the loan documentation follow a tiered scale similar to property transfer fees. MRTA (Mortgage Reducing Term Assurance) or MLTA (Mortgage Level Term Assurance) life insurance is typically required but can be sourced from third-party insurers. Valuation fees of RM200-1,000 depending on property value. Lock-in periods of 3-5 years are standard, with penalties of 2-3% of the outstanding amount for early full settlement during this period

## What Makes Malaysia's Mortgage Market Unique

Malaysia's flexi loan is a standout product — it functions like an overdraft secured against your property. Any excess payments immediately reduce the principal (and therefore interest) on a daily basis, and these excess amounts can be redrawn at any time without penalty. This makes it ideal for borrowers with fluctuating income or those who want to park emergency funds while reducing interest. The Islamic financing market is also highly developed, with products that mirror conventional loans in economic terms but comply with Shariah principles

## Refinancing Your Mortgage

Refinancing is common after the lock-in period expires (typically 3-5 years). Compare rates across multiple banks — Public Bank, CIMB, and Maybank are typically competitive. Factor in legal fees, stamp duty (0.5%), and valuation costs. The savings must exceed the switching costs to make refinancing worthwhile — typically a rate difference of 0.5%+ justifies switching. Many banks offer special refinancing packages with reduced fees or fee waivers

## Government Schemes and Support

Stamp duty exemption for first-time buyers on properties up to RM500,000 — full exemption on both the instrument of transfer and loan agreement. EPF Account 2 withdrawals can be used for property deposits, monthly instalments, and reducing loan principal. The MyFirst Home Scheme provides 100% financing guarantee for first-time buyers earning under RM5,000/month purchasing properties up to RM500,000. BSH (Bantuan Sara Hidup) provides cost-of-living assistance for lower-income households

Check our [government grants guide](/malaysia/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the latest programmes and eligibility details.

## Tips for Getting the Best Deal

Choose a flexi loan if you can make extra payments — the daily interest calculation means every ringgit of prepayment immediately saves you interest. Check your CCRIS and CTOS reports before applying and settle any outstanding issues. EPF Account 2 is a powerful tool — use it strategically for the deposit or to reduce your principal periodically. Compare Islamic and conventional products from the same bank — rates are often identical, but terms may differ slightly. Always negotiate the spread with the bank — the advertised rate is rarely the best available
