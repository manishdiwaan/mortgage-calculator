## Understanding the Belgium Property Market

The property market in Belgium offers diverse opportunities across its key markets. Brussels, Antwerp, Ghent, and Liège represent the main buying hotspots, with prices and demand varying significantly between regions.

The European Central Bank sets the benchmark interest rate, which influences mortgage pricing across all lenders. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=be) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

## Property Prices and Key Markets

Typical prices in Belgium range from €200,000 for apartments in smaller cities to €500,000+ in central Brussels.

Belgium's market is split between three regions — Flanders, Wallonia, and Brussels — each with different registration duties, tax incentives, and market dynamics. Flanders recently reformed its registration duty to a flat 3% for primary residences (down from 6%), making it significantly cheaper for owner-occupiers

## Setting Your Budget

Beyond the purchase price, you need to budget for transaction costs that typically add 10-15% in Wallonia/Brussels, 5-8% in Flanders to the total. Your deposit requirement is 10-20%, though this varies by lender and your buyer profile.

Registration duties range from 6% in Flanders to 12.5% in Wallonia and Brussels, making region choice a major cost factor

## Getting Finance Approved

Mortgage terms in Belgium extend up to 15-25 years, with 20-year terms most common. Lenders typically offer up to Historically up to 90-100%, but National Bank guidelines now recommend maximum 90% LTV for primary residences, with 80% for non-first-time buyers loan-to-value, meaning you need at least the remainder as a deposit.

Compare rates across major lenders including BNP Paribas Fortis, KBC, ING Belgium, Belfius, and AXA Bank. Our [mortgage types guide](/belgium/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) explains the differences between available loan products.

## Finding and Securing Property

Working with a qualified estate agent (immokantoor/agence immobilière) is the standard approach in Belgium. Research local areas thoroughly, attend viewings, and consider factors like transport links, amenities, and future development plans.

## Legal Process and Due Diligence

Property transfers in Belgium require a qualified notary (notaris/notaire) to handle the legal process. All property sales in Belgium must pass through a notary, who handles the deed transfer, title searches, and registration. The buyer and seller can use the same notary or appoint separate ones. The compromise (preliminary contract) is binding once signed, with a 10% deposit typically required.

Belgian homes are sold 'as seen' unless specific guarantees are included in the contract. A professional inspection is strongly recommended but not legally required

See our [property inspection checklist](/belgium/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) for what to check before committing.

## Government Support for Buyers

Flanders offers a reduced 3% registration duty for primary residences. Brussels has an abatement scheme exempting the first €200,000 from registration duties on purchases up to €600,000. Wallonia offers reduced rates for modest homes. The housing bonus (woonbonus) tax deduction has been abolished in most regions but replaced with other incentives
