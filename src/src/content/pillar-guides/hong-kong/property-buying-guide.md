## Understanding the Hong Kong Property Market

The property market in Hong Kong offers diverse opportunities across its key markets. Hong Kong Island, Kowloon, New Territories, and Lantau Island represent the main buying hotspots, with prices and demand varying significantly between regions.

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority sets the benchmark interest rate, which influences mortgage pricing across all lenders. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=hk) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

## Property Prices and Key Markets

Typical prices in Hong Kong range from HK$4,000,000 for small apartments in New Territories to HK$15,000,000+ on Hong Kong Island.

Hong Kong is one of the most expensive property markets in the world relative to income, with average price-to-income ratios exceeding 20x. The market is dominated by a small number of major developers (Sun Hung Kai, CK Asset, Henderson Land, New World). Government subsidised housing through the Hong Kong Housing Authority serves a significant portion of the population

## Setting Your Budget

Beyond the purchase price, you need to budget for transaction costs that typically add 5-20% depending on stamp duty bracket and buyer status, plus legal fees, agent commission (1%), and mortgage insurance premium to the total. Your deposit requirement is 10-50% depending on property value and MIP eligibility, though this varies by lender and your buyer profile.

Ad Valorem Stamp Duty at Scale 2 rates for first-time permanent residents (HK$100 to 4.25%), or flat 15% for non-first-time buyers and non-permanent residents

Read our [buying costs guide](/hong-kong/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a detailed breakdown of all purchase-related expenses.

## Getting Finance Approved

Mortgage terms in Hong Kong extend up to 30 years, with 25-30 year terms most common. Lenders typically offer up to 50-60% without mortgage insurance, up to 80-90% with HKMC Mortgage Insurance Programme for eligible properties loan-to-value, meaning you need at least the remainder as a deposit.

Compare rates across major lenders including HSBC, Hang Seng Bank, Bank of China (Hong Kong), Standard Chartered, and Bank of East Asia. Our [mortgage types guide](/hong-kong/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) explains the differences between available loan products.

## Finding and Securing Property

Working with a qualified estate agent (licensed under the Estate Agents Authority) is the standard approach in Hong Kong. Research local areas thoroughly, attend viewings, and consider factors like transport links, amenities, and future development plans.

## Legal Process and Due Diligence

Property transfers in Hong Kong require a qualified solicitor (律師) to handle the legal process. Property purchases follow a structured process — the buyer signs a Provisional Agreement for Sale and Purchase and pays a preliminary deposit (typically 3-5%). Within 14 days, the Formal Agreement is signed with a further deposit (typically bringing total to 10%). The solicitor conducts title searches and handles the assignment/transfer. Completion typically takes 2-3 months.

Professional building inspections are recommended for older buildings. The seller provides a Property Information Form but buyers should independently verify building orders, unauthorised building works, and the building's maintenance condition through the Buildings Department

See our [property inspection checklist](/hong-kong/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) for what to check before committing.

## Government Support for Buyers

The Home Ownership Scheme (HOS) sells subsidised flats at 30-40% below market value to eligible residents. The Green Form Subsidised Home Ownership (GSH) scheme targets public housing tenants. The White Form Secondary Market Scheme (WFSM) allows eligible buyers to purchase HOS resale flats. The Mortgage Insurance Programme (MIP) allows higher LTV ratios up to 90%
