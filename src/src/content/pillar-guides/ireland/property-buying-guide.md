## Understanding the Ireland Property Market

The property market in Ireland offers diverse opportunities across its key markets. Dublin, Cork, Galway, Limerick, and Waterford represent the main buying hotspots, with prices and demand varying significantly between regions.

The European Central Bank sets the benchmark interest rate, which influences mortgage pricing across all lenders. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=ie) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

## Property Prices and Key Markets

Typical prices in Ireland range from €200,000 for properties outside Dublin to €450,000+ in Dublin.

Ireland's property market is characterised by strict Central Bank lending rules introduced in 2015 — first-time buyers need a minimum 10% deposit, while second-time buyers need 20%. The Help to Buy scheme partially offsets this for new builds. Gazumping is legal in Ireland, meaning a seller can accept a higher offer after agreeing to sell to you, until contracts are formally exchanged

## Setting Your Budget

Beyond the purchase price, you need to budget for transaction costs that typically add 3-5% including stamp duty (1%), legal fees, valuation, survey, and mortgage protection insurance to the total. Your deposit requirement is 10% minimum (Central Bank rules), though this varies by lender and your buyer profile.

Stamp duty is 1% on properties up to €1 million and 2% on the balance above €1 million for residential purchases

## Getting Finance Approved

Mortgage terms in Ireland extend up to 35 years, with 25-30 year terms most common. Lenders typically offer up to 90% for first-time buyers, 80% for second and subsequent buyers (Central Bank macroprudential rules). LTI capped at 4x gross income for first-time buyers, 3.5x for others loan-to-value, meaning you need at least the remainder as a deposit.

Compare rates across major lenders including Bank of Ireland, AIB (Allied Irish Banks), Permanent TSB, Avant Money, Finance Ireland, and ICS Mortgages. Our [mortgage types guide](/ireland/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) explains the differences between available loan products.

## Finding and Securing Property

Working with a qualified estate agent or auctioneer is the standard approach in Ireland. Research local areas thoroughly, attend viewings, and consider factors like transport links, amenities, and future development plans.

## Legal Process and Due Diligence

Property transfers in Ireland require a qualified solicitor to handle the legal process. The buyer's solicitor conducts title investigations, raises requisitions on title, and reviews the contract for sale. The buyer signs the contract and pays a booking deposit (typically €5,000-€10,000) followed by the balance of 10% on contract exchange. Closing typically takes 4-8 weeks from contract signing. The Property Registration Authority handles land registry transfers.

Professional building surveys are strongly recommended in Ireland, particularly for older properties. A structural engineer's report (Type 2 or Type 3 survey) can identify issues ranging from pyrite contamination to structural defects. The BER (Building Energy Rating) certificate is legally required for all property sales

## Government Support for Buyers

The Help to Buy (HTB) scheme provides a tax refund of up to €30,000 for first-time buyers purchasing or building a new home (the refund equals the lesser of €30,000, 10% of purchase price, or income tax/DIRT paid over the previous 4 years). The First Home Scheme provides shared equity support of up to 30% for new builds. The Local Authority Home Loan Scheme offers low-interest mortgages for lower-income applicants
