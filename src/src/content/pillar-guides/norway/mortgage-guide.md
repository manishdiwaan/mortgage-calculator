## How Mortgages Work in Norway

Norges Bank's styringsrente (key policy rate) directly influences the floating mortgage rates offered by Norwegian banks. Norway is not an EU member and sets its own monetary policy based on domestic inflation and economic conditions. Norwegian mortgage rates are generally competitive, benefiting from a well-capitalised banking system and strong regulatory framework. The Finanstilsynet (Financial Supervisory Authority) sets lending standards including LTV and stress test requirements

## Types of Mortgages Available

The dominant product is the flytende rente (floating rate), chosen by approximately 80-90% of Norwegian borrowers. Fixed-rate products (fastrente) are available for 3, 5, or 10-year periods but are less popular due to historically higher pricing and the Norwegian preference for flexibility. Rammelån (line-of-credit/equity line) allows borrowing against existing property equity for renovation or other purposes. Boliglån (standard home loan) and førstehjemslån (first-home loan through Husbanken) are the main purchase products

## How Much Can You Borrow?

The Finanstilsynet requires banks to stress test borrowers at 3 percentage points above the current rate (or a minimum 7% rate, whichever is higher). Total debt cannot exceed 5 times gross annual income (gjeldsgrad). Monthly housing costs after the stress test should not leave the household unable to cover normal living expenses

The maximum loan-to-value in Norway is Maximum 85% LTV (Finanstilsynet regulation). In Oslo, a stricter requirement applies to secondary/investment properties — maximum 60% LTV. The remaining 15% must be genuine equity (savings, not other borrowed funds). Banks can make exceptions for up to 10% of new lending each quarter. Loan terms extend up to Typically 20-30 years, with 25 years being standard. Maximum term is generally 30 years. Norwegian mortgages are fully amortising — interest-only periods are generally not permitted for new loans under Finanstilsynet regulations.

## The Application Process

Apply through a bank or via a municipality for Husbanken startlån. Provide legitimasjon (ID), skattemelding (tax return), lønnslipper (payslips — last 3 months), documentation of savings and debts, and the kjøpekontrakt (purchase contract). The bank conducts a property valuation (often using automated models). Finansieringsbevis (financing certificate/pre-approval) is essential before bidding on properties, as the budrunde (bidding process) is fast-moving. Approval takes 1-5 days for standard cases

## Costs and Fees

Tinglysingsgebyr (registration fee) of kr 500-600 for the mortgage. Dokumentavgift (document duty) of 2.5% applies to the property purchase (not the mortgage) but is exempt for first-time buyers and borettslag (cooperative) purchases. Banks generally do not charge processing fees for standard mortgages. Early repayment of floating-rate mortgages carries no penalty. Fixed-rate loans may carry a breakage cost based on remaining term and rate differential

## What Makes Norway's Mortgage Market Unique

Norway's transparent budrunde (bidding process) means you need finansieringsbevis (pre-approval) before you can bid — no conditional bids are accepted. The 5x income debt cap is strictly enforced, meaning your total debt (mortgage plus all other debts including student loans) cannot exceed 5 times your gross annual income. This, combined with the 85% LTV limit and stress test at current rate plus 3%, creates one of the most conservative lending frameworks in Europe, but also one of the most stable housing markets

## Refinancing Your Mortgage

Floating-rate mortgages can be repaid or refinanced at any time without penalty, making switching banks straightforward. Banks actively compete for refinancing customers with competitive offers. Compare rates annually — even a 0.1-0.2% reduction saves significantly on Norwegian property values. Fixed-rate loans carry breakage costs if terminated early. Many borrowers negotiate rate reductions with their existing bank (prutesamtale) before going through the full switching process

## Government Schemes and Support

First-time buyers are exempt from the 2.5% dokumentavgift. Husbanken provides startlån (start loans) through municipalities for buyers who cannot qualify for conventional bank financing — typically young buyers, single parents, and lower-income families. BSU (boligsparing for ungdom) was a tax-advantaged savings scheme for under-34s with a 20% tax deduction on savings up to kr 27,500/year. Check current status of BSU as terms have been debated. Some municipalities offer tomtestøtte (discounted building plots) to attract residents

Check our [government grants guide](/norway/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the latest programmes and eligibility details.

## Tips for Getting the Best Deal

Get your finansieringsbevis before starting your property search — without it, you cannot participate in the budrunde. The 5x income cap means reducing existing debts (especially studielån) directly increases your borrowing capacity. Negotiate with your bank annually — Norwegian banks expect prutesamtaler (negotiation conversations) and often match competing offers without requiring you to switch. Choose floating rate unless you specifically want to lock in — historically, floating has been cheaper over time in Norway
