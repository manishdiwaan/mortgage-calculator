## Understanding the UAE Property Market

The property market in UAE offers diverse opportunities across its key markets. Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, and Ras Al Khaimah represent the main buying hotspots, with prices and demand varying significantly between regions.

The Central Bank of the UAE sets the benchmark interest rate, which influences mortgage pricing across all lenders. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=ae) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

## Property Prices and Key Markets

Typical prices in UAE range from AED 500,000 for apartments in emerging areas to AED 3,000,000+ in premium Dubai locations like Palm Jumeirah and Downtown.

The UAE property market is heavily driven by off-plan sales, with developers offering attractive payment plans — sometimes as low as 1% per month during construction. The market is also unique in being zero-income-tax, which makes property investment attractive for international buyers. Dubai allows 100% foreign freehold ownership in designated areas, a reform that transformed the market from 2002 onwards

## Setting Your Budget

Beyond the purchase price, you need to budget for transaction costs that typically add 7-9% including DLD transfer fee (4%), agent commission (2%), NOC fee, mortgage registration (0.25%), and trustee fee to the total. Your deposit requirement is 20-25% for residents, 30-50% for non-residents, though this varies by lender and your buyer profile.

Dubai charges 4% DLD (Dubai Land Department) transfer fee, split equally between buyer and seller (though negotiable). Abu Dhabi charges 2% registration fee. Oqood registration fee of 4% applies for off-plan properties in Dubai

## Getting Finance Approved

Mortgage terms in UAE extend up to 25 years, with maximum borrower age of 65 (salaried) or 70 (self-employed) at maturity. Lenders typically offer up to 80% for UAE nationals (first property), 75% for expat residents (first property under AED 5M), and 65-70% for non-residents. Off-plan LTV capped at 50% loan-to-value, meaning you need at least the remainder as a deposit.

Compare rates across major lenders including Emirates NBD, Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank (ADCB), Dubai Islamic Bank, Mashreq Bank, HSBC UAE, Standard Chartered UAE, and FAB (First Abu Dhabi Bank). ## Finding and Securing Property

Working with a qualified real estate agent (registered with RERA — Real Estate Regulatory Agency in Dubai, or DED in other emirates) is the standard approach in UAE. Research local areas thoroughly, attend viewings, and consider factors like transport links, amenities, and future development plans.

## Legal Process and Due Diligence

Property transfers in UAE require a qualified property lawyer (not mandatory but recommended, especially for non-residents) to handle the legal process. In Dubai, the process begins with signing a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU/Form F) through the agent. The buyer obtains a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the developer. The transfer is completed at the Dubai Land Department (DLD) trustee office, where both parties attend and the new title deed is issued. The process is increasingly digital through the Dubai REST app. Abu Dhabi and other emirates have similar but distinct processes.

For ready properties, commission a professional snagging inspection. For off-plan, review the developer's RERA registration and escrow account details. Check the DEWA (Dubai Electricity and Water Authority) connection status and service charges from the community management company. In Dubai, the DLD provides a property verification service

## Government Support for Buyers

The UAE does not have traditional buyer subsidy schemes since there is no income tax. However, the Mohammed bin Rashid Housing Establishment provides housing for UAE nationals in Dubai, and the Abu Dhabi Housing Authority offers similar programmes for Emiratis. The Golden Visa programme provides 10-year residency for property investments of AED 2 million+, adding immigration value to property purchases
