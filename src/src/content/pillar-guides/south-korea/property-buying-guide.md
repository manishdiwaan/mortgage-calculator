## Understanding the South Korea Property Market

South Korea's property market is one of the most dynamic in Asia-Pacific. The market is dominated by apartment complexes (아파트), which account for over 60% of housing in urban areas. Seoul, in particular, has seen significant price growth over the past decade, driven by limited supply and strong demand from young professionals and families.

The Bank of Korea sets the base rate, which directly influences mortgage pricing across all Korean lenders. As of 2026, the Korean government actively regulates the housing market through LTV (loan-to-value) and DTI (debt-to-income) caps, which vary by region and property type. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=kr) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

Beyond Seoul, cities like Busan, Incheon, Daejeon, and Daegu offer more affordable entry points. The Korean government has designated certain areas as speculative zones, which carry stricter lending and tax rules to cool price growth.

## The Jeonse System — Unique to Korea

South Korea has a unique rental system called jeonse (전세), where tenants pay a large lump-sum deposit (typically 50-80% of the property value) instead of monthly rent. This system is important for buyers because many people use jeonse deposits as a stepping stone toward full ownership. Understanding jeonse is essential, even for buyers, because it affects market dynamics and property valuations. The alternative rental system, wolse (월세), involves smaller deposits with monthly payments, similar to Western renting.

## Setting Your Budget

Property prices in Seoul can range from ₩300 million for a small apartment in outer districts to over ₩2 billion in premium Gangnam locations. Outside Seoul, prices drop significantly — Busan and Daejeon apartments start from ₩150-250 million.

Beyond the purchase price, budget for these additional costs. Acquisition tax ranges from 1% to 3.5% depending on property value and whether it is your first home. Registration tax and education tax add roughly 0.2-0.4%. Real estate agent commission is regulated by law and ranges from 0.3% to 0.9% depending on the transaction value. Legal and administrative fees for the transfer process typically run ₩500,000-2,000,000. Read our [buying costs guide](/south-korea/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a full breakdown.

## Getting Finance Approved

Korean banks offer mortgage terms of 10-40 years, with most buyers choosing 20-30 year terms. Fixed-rate, variable-rate, and mixed-rate products are available. The government's LTV regulations are critical — in speculative overheating zones, LTV is capped at 20-40%, meaning you need a larger deposit. In non-speculative areas, LTV can reach 60-70%.

The DTI (debt-to-income) ratio is also regulated. In Seoul, your total debt repayments generally cannot exceed 40% of your annual income. Foreigners can obtain mortgages from Korean banks, though the process requires additional documentation including visa status and proof of income.

Compare rates across major lenders including KB Kookmin, Shinhan, Hana, Woori, and NH NongHyup. Our [mortgage types guide](/south-korea/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) explains the differences between fixed, variable, and mixed-rate products.

## Finding and Securing Property

Most property searches begin on platforms like Naver Real Estate (네이버 부동산) or Zigbang. Licensed real estate agents (공인중개사) handle viewings and negotiations — their commission is regulated by local government. Always verify the agent's license before engaging their services.

Once you find a property, the process typically follows these steps. First, you agree on a price and pay a deposit of 5-10% to secure the property. Second, arrange your mortgage pre-approval. Third, conduct due diligence including checking the property registry (등기부등본) for any liens, encumbrances, or ownership disputes. Fourth, sign the sales contract. See our [inspection checklist](/south-korea/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) for what to check.

## Legal Process and Registration

Property transfers in South Korea are handled through the local district court registry office. You will need a judicial scrivener (법무사) or attorney to handle the registration. The buyer must register the transfer within 60 days of the contract date.

Key documents include the sales contract, identification, proof of funds, property registry extract, and building ledger. For foreigners, additional documents include your Alien Registration Card and visa documentation. Engage qualified legal representation for the entire process — never skip due diligence or registry checks.

## Government Support for Buyers

The Korean government periodically introduces support programmes for first-time buyers, including reduced acquisition tax rates, special mortgage products through the Korea Housing Finance Corporation (HF), and subsidised interest rates for young buyers under 40.
