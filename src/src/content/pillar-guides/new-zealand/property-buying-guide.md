## Understanding the New Zealand Property Market

The property market in New Zealand offers diverse opportunities across its key markets. Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Hamilton, and Tauranga represent the main buying hotspots, with prices and demand varying significantly between regions.

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand sets the benchmark interest rate, which influences mortgage pricing across all lenders. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=nz) to estimate repayments based on current rates.

## Property Prices and Key Markets

Typical prices in New Zealand range from $500,000 for properties in regional areas to $1,000,000+ in central Auckland.

New Zealand abolished stamp duty decades ago, making it one of the few developed markets with zero transfer taxes. The market is highly influenced by the RBNZ's LVR (loan-to-value ratio) restrictions, which limit the percentage of high-LVR lending banks can do. The Bright-line test taxes capital gains on investment properties sold within specified holding periods

## Setting Your Budget

Beyond the purchase price, you need to budget for transaction costs that typically add 2-4% including solicitor/conveyancer fees, building inspection, LIM report, and moving costs. No stamp duty to the total. Your deposit requirement is 20% standard, 5% minimum with low-deposit schemes, though this varies by lender and your buyer profile.

No stamp duty or transfer tax in New Zealand — one of the major cost advantages for buyers

## Getting Finance Approved

Mortgage terms in New Zealand extend up to 30 years, with 25-30 year terms most common. Lenders typically offer up to Standard 80% LTV. First-home buyers may access 90-95% LTV through the First Home Loan scheme. RBNZ imposes speed limits on the percentage of high-LVR lending each bank can do loan-to-value, meaning you need at least the remainder as a deposit.

Compare rates across major lenders including ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac, Kiwibank (state-owned), plus SBS Bank and TSB Bank. Our [mortgage types guide](/new-zealand/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) explains the differences between available loan products.

## Finding and Securing Property

Working with a qualified real estate agent (licensed under the REAA) is the standard approach in New Zealand. Research local areas thoroughly, attend viewings, and consider factors like transport links, amenities, and future development plans.

## Legal Process and Due Diligence

Property transfers in New Zealand require a qualified solicitor or conveyancer to handle the legal process. New Zealand uses a conditional offer system. The buyer submits a Sale and Purchase Agreement with conditions (typically finance, building inspection, LIM report, and due diligence). Once conditions are met, the agreement becomes unconditional. The solicitor handles title searches, settlement, and registration with Land Information New Zealand (LINZ). Settlement is typically 20-30 working days.

Building inspections are standard and highly recommended. A LIM (Land Information Memorandum) report from the local council reveals resource consents, building permits, hazard zones, and rates. Moisture testing is particularly important in New Zealand due to the leaky building crisis that affected homes built between 1994 and 2004

## Government Support for Buyers

First Home Grants provide up to $5,000 per person ($10,000 for couples) toward new builds and $3,000 per person for existing homes, for buyers meeting income and house price caps. KiwiSaver first-home withdrawal allows accessing your KiwiSaver retirement savings for a deposit. The First Home Loan scheme (formerly Welcome Home Loan) enables 5% deposits through participating lenders with underwriting support from Kāinga Ora
