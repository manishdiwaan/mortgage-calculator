## How Mortgages Work in Spain

Spanish mortgage rates follow ECB benchmarks. Variable rates are linked to the Euribor (typically the 12-month rate), while fixed rates are based on euro swap rates. Following the Euribor surge in 2022-2023, Spain saw a massive shift from variable to fixed-rate mortgages. The Banco de España supervises lending practices. The 2019 Mortgage Law (Ley de Hipotecas) significantly strengthened consumer protections, including caps on default interest and transparent cost allocation

## Types of Mortgages Available

The main products are hipoteca a tipo variable (variable rate — Euribor plus a spread, reviewing annually or semi-annually), hipoteca a tipo fijo (fixed rate for the full term — now the most popular product after the Euribor spike), and hipoteca a tipo mixto (mixed — fixed for an initial 3-10 year period then variable). The 2019 Mortgage Law established that most transaction costs (notary, registry, stamp duty on the loan) are paid by the bank, not the borrower — a significant consumer benefit

## How Much Can You Borrow?

Banks assess the cuota/ingresos (instalment-to-income) ratio — monthly payments should not exceed 30-35% of net household income. Stress testing at Euribor + 2-3% is standard for variable-rate applications. Employment stability (antigüedad laboral) and contract type (indefinido/permanent vs temporal/temporary) significantly impact approval prospects. CIRBE (Banco de España central credit register) records all existing credit exposures

The maximum loan-to-value in Spain is Typically 70-80% for residents, 50-70% for non-residents. Some banks offer up to 90% for young first-time buyers in specific programmes. Investment properties typically max out at 60-70% LTV. The difference between 80% and lower LTV often brings a significant rate improvement. Loan terms extend up to Up to 30 years, with maximum borrower age of 75 at maturity. Terms of 20-30 years are most common. Shorter fixed-rate terms (15-20 years) often get better rates.

## The Application Process

Start with a simulación (simulation) online or at the bank. Provide DNI/NIE (ID), última declaración de la renta (tax return), últimas 3 nóminas (payslips), vida laboral (employment history from Social Security), bank statements (3-6 months), nota simple of the property, and the arras contract. The bank orders a tasación (property valuation) by a certified appraiser. The FEIN (European Standardised Information Sheet) details all loan terms and must be provided at least 10 days before signing. The hipoteca is signed before a notary

## Costs and Fees

Under the 2019 law, the bank pays notary fees, registry fees, and AJD (stamp duty) on the mortgage. The buyer pays only the tasación (€250-400) and their half of the notary for the property deed. Comisión de apertura (opening commission) is increasingly rare (abolished for variable-rate mortgages under the new law). Comisión por amortización anticipada (early repayment) is capped at 0.25% (variable) or 2% (fixed, reducing over time). Vinculaciones (linked products like insurance, payroll domiciliation) reduce the rate but commit you to the bank's products

## What Makes Spain's Mortgage Market Unique

Spain's 2019 Mortgage Law (Ley 5/2019) is one of the strongest consumer protection frameworks in Europe. It shifted most mortgage costs to the bank, required a 10-day mandatory reflection period, regulated vinculaciones (banks cannot require you to buy their insurance, only offer it), and capped early repayment charges. The notary is required to meet with the borrower separately (acta de transparencia) at least one day before signing to verify they understand the terms — a level of consumer protection unique to Spain

## Refinancing Your Mortgage

Novación (renegotiation with your current bank) is the cheapest option — it modifies existing terms without cancelling the mortgage. Subrogación (transferring to a new bank) is the next option — the new bank handles the process and most costs. Cancelación y nueva constitución (cancel and take a new mortgage) is the most flexible but most expensive option. After the Euribor spike, many Spanish borrowers successfully converted from variable to fixed through novación or subrogación. Early repayment penalties are capped at 0.25% for variable and 2% for fixed (reducing over the term)

## Government Schemes and Support

Limited national schemes exist, but autonomous communities offer various programmes. Some regions reduce ITP (transfer tax) for first-time buyers, young buyers, large families, or purchases in depopulated areas. The ICO has introduced aval público (public guarantee) programmes enabling first-time buyers under 35 to access higher LTV mortgages with government backing. Plan de Vivienda provides subsidies in certain regions. Check your autonomous community's specific programmes

## Tips for Getting the Best Deal

Use iAhorro or HelpMyCash to compare offers — Spanish rates vary significantly between lenders. Negotiate vinculaciones carefully — domiciling your payroll and buying insurance from the bank reduces your rate but may not be the cheapest overall. The 10-day mandatory reflection period is your right — use it to compare your FEIN with other offers. If on a variable rate and struggling with Euribor increases, contact your bank about novación to fixed rate. Always get the acta de transparencia before the notary signing
