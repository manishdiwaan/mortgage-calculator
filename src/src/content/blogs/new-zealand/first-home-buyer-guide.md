## Your First Home in New Zealand — Where to Start

Buying your first home in New Zealand is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in New Zealand require a deposit of 5-20% of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Join KiwiSaver immediately if you haven't — it combines your savings, employer contributions, and government contributions into a powerful first-home fund. Contribute at least 3% to get the full employer match. After 3 years of membership, you can withdraw for a first home

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

First Home Grant provides up to $5,000/person ($10,000/couple) for new builds, $3,000/person for existing homes. KiwiSaver first-home withdrawal lets you access your retirement savings. The First Home Loan enables 5% deposits through Kāinga Ora. Combined, these can provide $20,000-60,000+ toward your first home

Check our [government grants guide](/new-zealand/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In New Zealand, the process works as follows.

Get pre-approval from one of the five main banks (ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac, Kiwibank). Pre-approval lasts 3-6 months. If using the First Home Loan, apply through a participating lender with Kāinga Ora underwriting support

Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=nz) to model different scenarios before applying. Read our [mortgage types guide](/new-zealand/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) to understand which loan product suits your situation.

## Understanding the Full Cost of Buying

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

No stamp duty or transfer tax — New Zealand is one of the few developed markets with zero transaction tax, a major cost advantage. Read our [buying costs guide](/new-zealand/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a complete breakdown of every expense.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not joining KiwiSaver early enough (3-year membership required before withdrawal), not splitting the mortgage across multiple fixed terms (exposing yourself to single-date renewal risk), waiving the building inspection in a hot market, and not checking for weathertightness issues in 1994-2004 era homes

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/new-zealand/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.

The typical first home purchase follows these stages: establish your budget and savings target, explore government support options, get pre-approved for financing, search for the right property, make an offer, complete legal due diligence and inspections, and finalise settlement.
