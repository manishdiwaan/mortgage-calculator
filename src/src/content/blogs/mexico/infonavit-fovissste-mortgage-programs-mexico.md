## How Infonavit and Fovissste Actually Work — A Practical Guide for Mexican Home Buyers

For most salaried workers in Mexico, the path to homeownership doesn't start at a bank — it starts with a housing fund they've been contributing to since their first formal paycheck. Yet despite millions of Mexicans holding active Infonavit or Fovissste accounts, a surprising number reach the point of buying a home without fully understanding how these institutions work, what their money can actually do, or how to avoid costly mistakes in the application process.

This guide cuts through the bureaucratic language and gives you a clear, honest picture of both programs in 2026 — including their real limitations, the products designed for higher-value properties, and what happens when your government credit alone isn't enough.

---

## What Are Infonavit and Fovissste, and Why Do They Exist?

Mexico operates a dual housing finance system rooted in its labor law. Under Article 123 of the Mexican Constitution and the Federal Labor Law (*Ley Federal del Trabajo*), employers are required to contribute **5% of each worker's salary** into a housing fund on a bimonthly basis.

- **Infonavit** (Instituto del Fondo Nacional de la Vivienda para los Trabajadores) serves workers employed in the private sector.
- **Fovissste** (Fondo de la Vivienda del Instituto de Seguridad y Servicios Sociales de los Trabajadores del Estado) serves federal government employees.

These are not voluntary savings accounts — they are mandatory, employer-funded contributions that accumulate over your working life. In 2026, Infonavit manages accounts for over **23 million active workers**, making it the single largest mortgage lender in Mexico by volume.

The critical distinction: these funds belong to you, but you can only access them through specific housing-related products. You cannot withdraw the balance as cash (with limited exceptions at retirement age).

## Understanding Your Infonavit Credit Capacity

### How Eligibility Is Calculated

Infonavit doesn't use a simple income threshold. Instead, it calculates your **Subcuenta de Vivienda** (housing subaccount balance) and assigns you a **puntuación** (point score) that determines your creditworthiness.

Your score is based on:
- **Age** (younger workers score higher)
- **Salary** (higher net salary = more points)
- **Continuous employment** (uninterrupted formal employment with the same or different employer)
- **Account balance** (your accumulated savings in the Subcuenta de Vivienda)

As of 2026, the minimum score required to apply for most Infonavit products is **1,080 points**. You can check your current score at any time through the **Portal Infonavit** (portalinfonavit.org.mx) using your CURP and NSS (Número de Seguridad Social).

### Maximum Loan Amounts in 2026

Infonavit loan limits are expressed in **VSM** (veces el salario mínimo) but translated into pesos based on the current minimum wage. In 2026, the general maximum credit for an Infonavit loan varies by income bracket, but the standard ceiling for traditional **Crédito Infonavit** sits around **$1,800,000 MXN** for workers earning above 11 times the minimum wage (UMAs).

For properties in higher-cost markets — Mexico City (CDMX), Monterrey, Guadalajara, and Los Cabos — this ceiling is often insufficient for the median property price. This is precisely where bridge products become essential.

## Infonavit's Key Credit Products

### Crédito Infonavit Traditional

The standard product. You borrow directly from Infonavit, make monthly payments to the institute, and the loan is denominated in **VSM** (salary minimum units), which means your balance adjusts with minimum wage increases. This indexing is a critical point many buyers overlook — your outstanding balance can grow faster than your monthly payment reduces it if wage inflation runs hot.

**Best for:** Buyers purchasing properties priced under $1,500,000 MXN in mid-tier markets such as Querétaro, Mérida, or Puebla.

### Cofinavit (Combined Financing)

**Cofinavit** is a joint credit product where Infonavit and a participating commercial bank (BBVA, Banorte, HSBC, Santander, Scotiabank, and others) co-finance the purchase. Infonavit provides a portion of the loan, the bank provides the rest, and you make a single monthly payment to the bank.

This is the most commonly used product for mid-to-upper market purchases in 2026. It allows buyers to access properties priced between **$1,500,000 and $5,000,000 MXN** by layering institutional credit with commercial bank financing.

**Key requirement:** You must meet the bank's independent credit approval criteria in addition to Infonavit's scoring threshold.

### Infonavit Total

A newer product designed for workers with higher incomes who want to use their Infonavit account as a down payment supplement while financing the bulk through a commercial bank. Under Infonavit Total, your Subcuenta balance is applied directly toward the purchase price or closing costs.

### Crédito Seguro

Targeted at workers who are self-employed or have informal income in addition to formal employment. Launched as part of Infonavit's financial inclusion push, this product accepts supplementary income documentation to increase the loan amount available.

## Understanding Fovissste: The Federal Worker's Equivalent

Fovissste operates on similar principles but with some important structural differences that matter enormously in practice.

### The Sorteo vs. Puntuaje System

Historically, Fovissste allocated credits through an **annual lottery (sorteo)**, meaning eligible workers submitted applications and credits were distributed by random draw. This created significant frustration — workers could be eligible for years without receiving a credit.

In recent years, Fovissste has expanded its **puntuaje** (points-based) system, which allows workers above a certain score to access credit without waiting for the lottery. Check your current status through **Mi Cuenta Fovissste** on the official ISSSTE portal.

### Alia2 Plus and Respalda2

Fovissste offers bridge products similar to Cofinavit:

- **Alia2 Plus**: Combines Fovissste credit with a commercial bank loan, functioning similarly to Cofinavit for federal workers.
- **Respalda2**: Allows a Fovissste-affiliated worker and an Infonavit-affiliated worker (e.g., a couple where one works in the private sector, one in government) to **combine both credits** for a single property purchase. This is one of the most underused but powerful tools available to mixed-employment households.

### Fovissste Loan Limits

Fovissste credit amounts in 2026 are generally higher than standard Infonavit limits for equivalent salary levels, with maximum credits reaching approximately **$2,200,000 MXN** for senior federal employees with long service records.

## Practical Steps: Applying for Your Credit in 2026

### Step 1: Verify Your Account Status

Before anything else, confirm your employer has been making consistent contributions. Gaps in contributions — which happen more often than employers admit — reduce your point score and can disqualify you. Use the Infonavit or Fovissste portal to review your contribution history. If you find discrepancies, file a **Aclaración de Aportaciones** immediately.

### Step 2: Pre-qualify for the Loan Amount

Use the official simulators on portalinfonavit.org.mx or the Fovissste site to estimate your credit ceiling before you begin property hunting. Going into negotiations without knowing your real budget is one of the most common and costly mistakes buyers make.

### Step 3: Engage a Perito Valuador (Certified Appraiser)

Infonavit and Fovissste require an official **avalúo** (property appraisal) conducted by a certified **perito valuador** from their approved roster. The property's appraised value — not the asking price — determines the maximum loan amount. In competitive markets like CDMX's Coyoacán or Monterrey's San Pedro Garza García, appraised values frequently come in 8–15% below market asking prices, directly affecting your financing ceiling.

### Step 4: Confirm the Property is on the Registro Único de Vivienda (RUV)

For new developments, the property must be registered with the **Registro Único de Vivienda** and typically needs to be built by a **Desarrollador Autorizado** (authorized developer). Infonavit maintains a verified list of approved developments at buenver.infonavit.org.mx.

### Step 5: Work With a Notario Público

In Mexico, all real estate transactions must be formalized through a **Notario Público** — a government-authorized legal professional with far broader authority than notaries in most other countries. Your Notario will handle title verification, tax calculations, and registration with the **Registro Público de la Propiedad**. Budget **$30,000–$80,000 MXN** in notarial fees depending on property value and state.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Changing jobs during the application process.** If you switch employers after submitting your Infonavit application, your point score can reset or your credit can be suspended. Timing a job change with a mortgage application is a serious risk.

**Underestimating complementary costs.** Infonavit and Fovissste credits cover the property price but not always closing costs, which typically run **5–8% of the purchase price** in Mexico. These must be covered from personal funds.

**Ignoring the VSM indexing risk.** If your Infonavit credit is denominated in VSM rather than fixed pesos, minimum wage increases (which have been substantial in recent years — over 20% annually between 2019–2024) increase your outstanding balance. Ask explicitly whether your product offers a **peso-fixed** rate or VSM indexing.

**Not checking for INFONAVIT's "Línea 2" eligibility.** Workers who have already paid off a previous Infonavit credit may be eligible for a **second loan (Crédito de Segunda Ocasión)**. This is frequently overlooked by workers who assume the benefit was a one-time opportunity.

## The Bigger Picture: Are These Programs Right for You?

Infonavit and Fovissste remain the most accessible mortgage products in Mexico for formal sector workers — no other institution offers comparable terms at the income levels they serve. However, they are not automatically the optimal choice for every buyer.

Workers with high incomes, strong credit histories, and the ability to qualify for competitive commercial bank rates (TIIE-linked products now available at **TIIE + 3–5%** from major banks) may find that **not using** their Infonavit credit for the mortgage and instead preserving the Subcuenta for retirement makes mathematical sense — particularly now that 2023 pension reforms improved the retirement benefit calculation.

Consult a **corredor hipotecario certificado** (certified mortgage broker) — look for members of the **Asociación Mexicana de Entidades de Ahorro y Crédito Popular (AMUCSS)** or brokers certified through the **Comisión Nacional para la Protección y Defensa de los Usuarios de Servicios Financieros (CONDUSEF)** — before committing to any product. A good broker runs the numbers across all available options, not just the most convenient one.

Your Infonavit or Fovissste credit is one of the most significant financial assets you've built through years of formal employment. Use it strategically.
