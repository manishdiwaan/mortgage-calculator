## Your First Home in Belgium — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Belgium is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Belgium require a deposit of 10-20% of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Open a spaarrekening (savings account) with a Belgian bank. Some banks offer preferential rates for housing savings. Consider reducing existing debts to improve your borrowing profile, as Belgian lenders look closely at your debt-to-income ratio

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

Flanders offers a reduced 3% registration duty for primary residences (down from 6%). Brussels provides an abatement on the first €200,000 for properties up to €600,000. These regional incentives can save first-time buyers €10,000-20,000

Check our [government grants guide](/belgium/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Belgium, the process works as follows.

Get a credit simulation from multiple banks. BNP Paribas Fortis, KBC, ING, and Belfius all offer online simulators. A mortgage broker (hypotheekmakelaar) can compare across lenders for you

Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=be) to model different scenarios before applying. Read our [mortgage types guide](/belgium/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) to understand which loan product suits your situation.

## Understanding the Full Cost of Buying

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

Registration duties are the largest cost: 3% in Flanders for primary residences, 12. Read our [buying costs guide](/belgium/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a complete breakdown of every expense.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not understanding the difference between registration duties across regions (6% Flanders vs 12.5% Wallonia/Brussels), underestimating notary fees (which include registration tax), forgetting schuldsaldoverzekering (outstanding balance insurance) costs, and skipping the building inspection because it is not legally required

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/belgium/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
