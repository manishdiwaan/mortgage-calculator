## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Belgium?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Belgium. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Registration duties are the largest cost: 3% in Flanders for primary residences, 12.5% in Wallonia and Brussels-Capital. Notary fees of 1-2% cover the deed transfer, title searches, and administrative costs. Mortgage registration tax is approximately 1% of the loan amount. Additional costs include building inspection (€300-500), energy performance certificate review, and moving expenses. Budget 5-8% in Flanders or 14-17% in Wallonia/Brussels on top of the purchase price

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

The schuldsaldoverzekering (outstanding balance insurance) is an often-overlooked cost that can run €2,000-10,000+ depending on age and health. Syndic fees for apartment buildings, annual property tax (onroerende voorheffing/précompte immobilier), and renovation costs for older Belgian properties can be substantial

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/belgium/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Belgium.

Flanders offers a reduced 3% registration duty for primary residences (down from 6%). Check our [government grants guide](/belgium/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=be) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
