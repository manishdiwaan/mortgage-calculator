## Your First Home in Thailand — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Thailand is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Thailand require a deposit of 10-20% of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Check GH Bank eligibility first — their subsidised rates are the best available for Thai nationals. Build savings for the deposit and closing costs. Maintain a clean NCB (National Credit Bureau) record — defaults significantly impact approval. Time your purchase during government fee reduction campaigns if possible

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

GH Bank provides below-market rates for Thai nationals purchasing affordable housing. Government periodically announces fee reduction campaigns reducing transfer and mortgage registration fees to 0.01%. First-time buyer programmes through GH Bank offer reduced deposits and rates

Check our [government grants guide](/thailand/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Thailand, the process works as follows.

Check GH Bank eligibility at ghbank.co.th first. For commercial banks, compare offers from Bangkok Bank, KBank, and SCB. Get pre-approval before property hunting. Ask specifically about first-time buyer promotional rates

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not checking GH Bank first (missing subsidised rates), not understanding the promotional vs reversion rate (the attractive initial rate typically lasts 1-3 years before reverting to a much higher MRR-based rate), buying a condominium without checking the 49% foreign ownership quota if applicable, and not planning to refinance when the promotional period ends

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/thailand/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
