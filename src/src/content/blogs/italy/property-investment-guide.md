## Property Investment Guide — Italy 2026

Property investment can build long-term wealth through rental income and capital appreciation. However, it requires careful analysis of yields, taxes, financing costs, and market dynamics. This guide covers everything you need to know about investing in property in Italy.

## Rental Yields and Market Overview

Italian rental yields average 4-7% depending on location — southern Italy and tourist areas offer higher yields. Short-term rentals (Airbnb) face increasing regulation with mandatory CIN codes. Cedolare secca (flat tax) of 21% on residential rental income (26% for short-term rentals from 2024) simplifies taxation. IMU (property tax) applies to non-primary residences at 0.76-1.06% of cadastral value. Capital gains tax at 26% if sold within 5 years (primary residence exempt)

## Financing Investment Property

Investment property financing differs from owner-occupier mortgages. Lenders typically require higher deposits (20-40%), charge higher interest rates (0.5-2% premium), and apply stricter affordability criteria. Rental income may be partially factored into the assessment, but expect conservative treatment. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=it) to model investment scenarios.

## Tax Implications

Understanding the full tax picture is critical for investment returns. Rental income taxation, capital gains treatment, deductible expenses, and stamp duty or transfer tax surcharges on additional properties all affect your net return. ## Due Diligence for Investment

Investment due diligence goes beyond a standard purchase. Research local rental demand, vacancy rates, tenant quality, maintenance requirements, and comparable rental values.
