## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Denmark?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Denmark. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Tinglysningsafgift (registration duty) of 0.6% of the purchase price plus kr 1,850 for property transfer. Mortgage registration of 1.45% of the loan amount plus kr 1,850. Lawyer fees of kr 10,000-25,000. Agent commission is paid by the seller. Tilstandsrapport (condition report) is typically provided by the seller. Ejerskifteforsikring (change-of-ownership insurance) of kr 5,000-20,000 is optional but recommended. Total buyer costs: 2-5%

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

Bidrag (administration fees) of 0.5-1.5% per year on the outstanding covered bond balance are an ongoing cost that significantly affects total mortgage cost over time. The bank top-up loan (for the 80-95% LTV portion) carries a much higher interest rate than the covered bond rate — budget accordingly. Ejendomsværdiskat (property value tax) is an annual cost based on the public valuation

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/denmark/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Denmark.

First-time buyers benefit from mortgage interest tax deductibility at approximately 33% for amounts up to kr 50,000 per person. Check our [government grants guide](/denmark/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=dk) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
