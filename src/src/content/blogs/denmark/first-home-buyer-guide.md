## Your First Home in Denmark — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Denmark is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Denmark require a deposit of 5% minimum of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Denmark does not have a specific first-time buyer savings scheme like other countries. Focus on building a 5-10% cash deposit while minimising existing debts. Consider BSU (Boligopsparing for Ungdom) if still available — a tax-advantaged savings account for young buyers. Maintain a stable employment record

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

First-time buyers benefit from mortgage interest tax deductibility at approximately 33% for amounts up to kr 50,000 per person. Some municipalities offer discounted building plots. Denmark's covered bond system provides some of the lowest fixed rates in Europe, giving first-time buyers access to affordable 30-year fixed-rate mortgages

Check our [government grants guide](/denmark/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Denmark, the process works as follows.

Contact a realkredit institute (Nykredit, Realkredit Danmark, or Totalkredit) and your bank for a combined financing plan. The realkredit provides up to 80% through covered bonds, and your bank provides the top-up loan. Get a tilsagn (commitment) before house hunting

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not understanding the two-layer financing structure (realkredit + bank loan at different rates), underestimating bidrag (administration fees) which are an ongoing percentage-based cost, rushing into the budrunde (bidding process) without financing in place, and not budgeting for the bank top-up loan which carries a higher rate than the covered bond portion

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/denmark/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
