## Property Investment Guide — Denmark 2026

Property investment can build long-term wealth through rental income and capital appreciation. However, it requires careful analysis of yields, taxes, financing costs, and market dynamics. This guide covers everything you need to know about investing in property in Denmark.

## Rental Yields and Market Overview

Danish rental yields average 3-5% in Copenhagen, higher in smaller cities. Rental income is taxed as personal income. Capital gains on investment property are taxed as personal income. The ejendomsværdiskat (property value tax) applies to all owned properties. Strict rent control (huslejenævn) in older Copenhagen apartments limits rental increases. Newer apartments have more flexible pricing

## Financing Investment Property

Investment property financing differs from owner-occupier mortgages. Lenders typically require higher deposits (20-40%), charge higher interest rates (0.5-2% premium), and apply stricter affordability criteria. Rental income may be partially factored into the assessment, but expect conservative treatment. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=dk) to model investment scenarios.

## Tax Implications

Understanding the full tax picture is critical for investment returns. Rental income taxation, capital gains treatment, deductible expenses, and stamp duty or transfer tax surcharges on additional properties all affect your net return. Our [property tax guide](/denmark/blog/property-tax-guide/) provides a comprehensive breakdown of all applicable taxes.

## Due Diligence for Investment

Investment due diligence goes beyond a standard purchase. Research local rental demand, vacancy rates, tenant quality, maintenance requirements, and comparable rental values. Our [property inspection checklist](/denmark/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers physical due diligence, while local rental market research is equally important.
