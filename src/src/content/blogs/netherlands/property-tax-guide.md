## Property Tax Guide — Netherlands 2026

Understanding the full tax picture is essential for any property purchase or investment. Taxes affect buying costs, ongoing ownership expenses, rental income, and eventual sale proceeds. This guide covers every tax that applies to property in Netherlands.

## Taxes When Buying

Overdrachtsbelasting: 0% for first-time buyers 18-35 (up to €510,000), 2% for other primary residences, 10.4% for investment. Hypotheekrenteaftrek (mortgage interest deduction) reduces taxable income. Eigenwoningforfait: imputed rental value of 0.35% of WOZ value is added to taxable income (partially offset by interest deduction). OZB (local property tax) varies by municipality — typically €200-600/year. Box 3 taxation for investment property based on deemed returns

## Ongoing Annual Taxes

Property ownership in Netherlands involves annual tax obligations beyond the one-off purchase taxes. These typically include local government property charges, which vary by area and property value. Budget for these as a fixed annual cost of ownership alongside insurance and maintenance.

## Taxes on Rental Income

If you rent out your property, the rental income is subject to taxation. The rules for what expenses can be deducted (mortgage interest, maintenance, management fees, depreciation, insurance) vary and can significantly affect your net return. Consult a tax advisor familiar with Netherlands's property tax rules for investment properties.

## Taxes When Selling

Capital gains treatment varies between primary residences and investment properties, with many jurisdictions offering preferential treatment or exemptions for primary residences held for a minimum period. Understanding the tax implications before you buy helps you plan your holding period and exit strategy.

## Tax Planning Tips

Work with a tax professional who understands property taxation in Netherlands. Structure your purchase correctly from the start — the ownership structure (personal, joint, corporate) affects tax treatment. Keep records of all improvements and costs as these may reduce future capital gains liability.
