## Your First Home in Netherlands — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Netherlands is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Netherlands require a deposit of 0% (100% financing available) of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

While 100% financing is available, you need savings for the kosten koper (buyer's costs) of approximately 4-6% — notary, transfer tax (if not exempt), valuation, and advisor fees. NHG guarantee cost of 0.6% is also upfront. Build this reserve before house hunting. Register with a hypotheekadviseur early

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

First-time buyers aged 18-35 are exempt from the 2% transfer tax on properties up to €510,000. NHG (Nationale Hypotheek Garantie) provides a government-backed guarantee for mortgages up to €435,000, offering lower rates and residual debt protection. These benefits combined can save €15,000-25,000+ on a typical first purchase

Check our [government grants guide](/netherlands/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Netherlands, the process works as follows.

Engage a hypotheekadviseur (mortgage advisor) — legally required and their independent advice is invaluable. They submit your application to selected lenders and manage the process. Get a pre-approval letter for confidence when making offers in the competitive Dutch market

## Understanding the Full Cost of Buying

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

Overdrachtsbelasting (transfer tax): 0% for first-time buyers 18-35 (up to €510,000), 2% for other primary residences, 10. Read our [buying costs guide](/netherlands/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a complete breakdown of every expense.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not using the first-time buyer transfer tax exemption (it only applies up to age 35 and up to €510,000), skipping the NHG guarantee for eligible purchases (missing the 0.3-0.6% rate discount), not budgeting for kosten koper (4-6% that must come from savings despite 100% financing), and not getting a bouwkundige keuring (building inspection) — increasingly required by lenders

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/netherlands/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
