## Property Tax Guide — Ireland 2026

Understanding the full tax picture is essential for any property purchase or investment. Taxes affect buying costs, ongoing ownership expenses, rental income, and eventual sale proceeds. This guide covers every tax that applies to property in Ireland.

## Taxes When Buying

Stamp duty of 1% up to €1M, 2% above (one of the lowest in Europe). LPT (Local Property Tax) based on value bands — typically €200-1,000+ annually. Rental income at marginal rate (up to 52%). Capital gains at 33% (with €1,270 annual exemption). PRSI of 4% on rental income. TRS (mortgage interest relief) was discontinued. Buildings and contents insurance are ongoing costs

## Ongoing Annual Taxes

Property ownership in Ireland involves annual tax obligations beyond the one-off purchase taxes. These typically include local government property charges, which vary by area and property value. Budget for these as a fixed annual cost of ownership alongside insurance and maintenance.

## Taxes on Rental Income

If you rent out your property, the rental income is subject to taxation. The rules for what expenses can be deducted (mortgage interest, maintenance, management fees, depreciation, insurance) vary and can significantly affect your net return. Consult a tax advisor familiar with Ireland's property tax rules for investment properties.

## Taxes When Selling

Capital gains treatment varies between primary residences and investment properties, with many jurisdictions offering preferential treatment or exemptions for primary residences held for a minimum period. Understanding the tax implications before you buy helps you plan your holding period and exit strategy.

## Tax Planning Tips

Work with a tax professional who understands property taxation in Ireland. Structure your purchase correctly from the start — the ownership structure (personal, joint, corporate) affects tax treatment. Keep records of all improvements and costs as these may reduce future capital gains liability.
