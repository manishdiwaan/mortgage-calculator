## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Ireland?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Ireland. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Stamp duty of 1% on properties up to €1 million (2% above that). Solicitor fees of €2,000-3,500 for the conveyance and mortgage. Valuation fee of €150-300. Engineers survey of €300-1,000 (strongly recommended). Mortgage protection insurance (mandatory — annual premium of €200-800). Buildings insurance from completion date. Total additional costs: 3-5%

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

Mortgage protection insurance is legally required in Ireland — a life policy covering the outstanding balance. This costs €200-800/year depending on age and health. BER (Building Energy Rating) upgrades may be needed for older properties. Property tax (LPT) is an annual charge based on the property's value band. Management fees for apartments of €1,000-3,000/year

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/ireland/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Ireland.

Help to Buy (HTB) provides a tax refund of up to €30,000 for first-time buyers of new homes. Check our [government grants guide](/ireland/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=ie) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
