## Your First Home in Switzerland — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Switzerland is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Switzerland require a deposit of 20% minimum (at least 10% non-pension) of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Build genuine savings of at least 10% (the remaining 10% can come from pension funds). Maximise Pillar 3a contributions (CHF 7,056/year for employees with Pillar 2) — these are tax-deductible and can be used for property purchase. Start saving early — Swiss property prices mean deposits are substantial

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

Pillar 2 (pension fund) and Pillar 3a (private retirement savings) can be used toward the 20% deposit — up to 10% from pension sources. Some cantons offer subsidised construction loans or reduced-rate mortgages for families. Wohneigentumsförderung allows pension fund withdrawals for residential property

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Switzerland, the process works as follows.

Use MoneyPark or VermögensZentrum — they compare across 100+ lenders including banks, insurance companies, and pension funds. The Tragbarkeit (affordability) test at 5% imputed interest is conservative — verify your maximum mortgage amount early

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not understanding the Tragbarkeit calculation (5% imputed rate means your theoretical maximum is much lower than actual payments), underestimating the Eigenmietwert tax implication (owning means your imputed rental income is taxed), not comparing across enough lenders (Swiss rate differences are 0.3-0.5%), and breaking a Festhypothek early (the penalty can be enormous)

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/switzerland/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
