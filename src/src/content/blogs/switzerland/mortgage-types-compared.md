## Choosing the Right Mortgage in Switzerland

Selecting the right mortgage type is one of the most important financial decisions in the buying process. The wrong choice can cost you tens of thousands over the life of the loan. This guide explains every mortgage type available in Switzerland and helps you choose.

## Types of Mortgages Available

Switzerland has a distinctive mortgage culture. **Festhypothek (fixed-rate)** locks your rate for 2-10 years — the most popular product, providing certainty for the chosen period. **Variable Hypothek** adjusts at the bank's discretion with notice — fully flexible but rate uncertain. **SARON-Hypothek** is linked to the Swiss Average Rate Overnight — transparent market-linked pricing that replaced the former LIBOR mortgage. Swiss mortgages operate in two tranches: the **first mortgage** (up to 65% LTV) can be maintained indefinitely with interest-only payments. The **second mortgage** (65-80% LTV) must be amortised within 15 years or by retirement age

## Head-to-Head Comparison

5-year and 10-year Festhypothek are most popular — balancing certainty with reasonable pricing. Shorter fixed periods (2-3 years) get better rates but require more frequent renewal decisions. SARON is the most transparent variable option — linked to the overnight rate rather than the bank's discretion. The two-tranche system means your first mortgage (up to 65% LTV) never needs to be repaid — only interest payments are required, which are tax-deductible

## Which Type Is Right for You?

Your ideal mortgage type depends on your risk tolerance, financial stability, and market outlook.

Choose **fixed rate** if you value payment certainty, are on a tight budget with little room for payment increases, or believe rates will rise. Choose **variable rate** if you can absorb payment increases, want the lowest possible initial cost, or believe rates will stay stable or fall.

Consider your loan term carefully. Shorter terms mean higher monthly payments but dramatically lower total interest. Our [mortgage guide](/switzerland/mortgage-guide/) provides a detailed analysis of how term length affects total cost.
