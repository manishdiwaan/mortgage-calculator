## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Hong Kong?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Hong Kong. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Ad Valorem Stamp Duty at Scale 2 rates for first-time permanent residents: HK$100 to 4.25% depending on value. Legal fees of HK$10,000-30,000 for the purchase and mortgage deeds. Agent commission of 1% of the purchase price. Mortgage insurance premium of 1.5-5% if using MIP for higher LTV. Total: 5-10% for first-time citizens, dramatically more for non-permanent residents (15% stamp duty)

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

The mortgage insurance premium (MIP) for higher LTV can be 1.5-5% of the loan — on a HK$6 million mortgage this is HK$90,000-300,000. Management fees for apartment buildings of HK$1,500-5,000/month are an ongoing cost. Government rates and rent add another HK$5,000-15,000/quarter

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/hong-kong/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Hong Kong.

The Home Ownership Scheme (HOS) sells flats at 30-40% below market value. Check our [government grants guide](/hong-kong/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=hk) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
