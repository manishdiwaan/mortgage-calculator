## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Singapore?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Singapore. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

BSD (Buyer's Stamp Duty) of 1-6% on the property value. HDB resale levy if you previously owned a subsidised flat. Conveyancing legal fees of $2,000-3,500. Option fee of $1,000 (for HDB) or up to 5% (for private). Renovation costs of $30,000-80,000 for HDB. Mortgage insurance (optional but recommended). Total excluding BSD: 2-4% plus renovation

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

CPF accrued interest is the biggest hidden cost — the 2.5% compounding interest on CPF amounts used for property must be refunded to CPF when the property is sold. Over 20-30 years, this can reduce net sale proceeds by $50,000-150,000. Cash-over-valuation (COV) for HDB resale must be paid in cash. Monthly conservancy charges of $50-100 for HDB or maintenance fees of $300-800 for condominiums

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/singapore/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Singapore.

CPF Housing Grants provide up to $80,000 for eligible first-time HDB resale buyers. Check our [government grants guide](/singapore/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=sg) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
