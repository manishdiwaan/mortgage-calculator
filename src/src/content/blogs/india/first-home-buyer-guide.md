## Your First Home in India — Where to Start

Buying your first home in India is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in India require a deposit of 10-25% of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Start a dedicated savings SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) in a liquid fund for your down payment. Reduce existing EMIs to improve your CIBIL score above 750. Check your CIBIL report free annually at cibil.com and dispute any errors

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

PMAY (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana) provides interest subsidy up to ₹2.67 lakh for eligible first-time buyers. Section 80C allows tax deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh on principal repayment. Section 24(b) provides up to ₹2 lakh deduction on interest for self-occupied property. Combined tax benefits can save ₹3-5 lakh in the first few years

Check our [government grants guide](/india/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In India, the process works as follows.

Get a home loan sanction letter from SBI, HDFC Bank, or ICICI Bank before property hunting. This confirms your maximum borrowing amount and gives you negotiating power with developers. Compare rates online at BankBazaar or PaisaBazaar

Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=in) to model different scenarios before applying. Read our [mortgage types guide](/india/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) to understand which loan product suits your situation.

## Understanding the Full Cost of Buying

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

Stamp duty of 3-8% depending on state — Maharashtra 5% (6% in Mumbai), Karnataka 5%, Delhi 4-6%, Tamil Nadu 7%. Read our [buying costs guide](/india/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a complete breakdown of every expense.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not checking RERA registration for under-construction properties, not comparing rates across at least 3-4 lenders, forgetting to budget for stamp duty and registration (7-12% in most states), accepting the first CIBIL check without improving your score first, and not using the balance transfer option when better rates are available

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/india/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
