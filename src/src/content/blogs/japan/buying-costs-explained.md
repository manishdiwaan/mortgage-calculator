## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Japan?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Japan. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Agent commission of up to 3% + ¥60,000 (plus consumption tax). Registration and licence taxes of 0.3-1.5% of the property value. Stamp duty (印紙税) on the contract based on transaction value. Loan guarantee fee of approximately 2% (for bank loans — Flat 35 does not charge this). Judicial scrivener fees of ¥100,000-200,000. Fire insurance is mandatory. Total additional costs: 6-10%

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

The loan guarantee fee of approximately 2% of the loan amount is a significant upfront cost for bank loans — some banks waive this but charge a higher interest rate instead. Annual fixed asset tax (固定資産税) and city planning tax (都市計画税) total approximately 1.4-1.7% of the assessed value. Apartment management fees (管理費) and repair fund contributions (修繕積立金) of ¥20,000-40,000/month are ongoing

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/japan/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Japan.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
