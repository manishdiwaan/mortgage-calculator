## Property Investment Guide — South Korea 2026

Property investment can build long-term wealth through rental income and capital appreciation. However, it requires careful analysis of yields, taxes, financing costs, and market dynamics. This guide covers everything you need to know about investing in property in South Korea.

## Rental Yields and Market Overview

Korean rental yields average 2-4% in Seoul, higher in secondary cities. The jeonse system means many investment properties are rented on a deposit basis rather than monthly rent. Comprehensive real estate tax (종합부동산세) applies to high-value holdings. Capital gains tax: 6-45% (plus additional 20-30% surcharge for multi-property owners in speculative zones). Acquisition tax of 1-12% depending on property count and value. Rental income tax at progressive rates

## Financing Investment Property

Investment property financing differs from owner-occupier mortgages. Lenders typically require higher deposits (20-40%), charge higher interest rates (0.5-2% premium), and apply stricter affordability criteria. Rental income may be partially factored into the assessment, but expect conservative treatment. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=kr) to model investment scenarios.

## Tax Implications

Understanding the full tax picture is critical for investment returns. Rental income taxation, capital gains treatment, deductible expenses, and stamp duty or transfer tax surcharges on additional properties all affect your net return. Our [property tax guide](/south-korea/blog/property-tax-guide/) provides a comprehensive breakdown of all applicable taxes.

## Due Diligence for Investment

Investment due diligence goes beyond a standard purchase. Research local rental demand, vacancy rates, tenant quality, maintenance requirements, and comparable rental values. Our [property inspection checklist](/south-korea/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers physical due diligence, while local rental market research is equally important.
