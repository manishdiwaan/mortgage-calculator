## Your First Home in South Korea — Where to Start

Buying your first home in South Korea is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in South Korea require a deposit of 30-80% depending on zone of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Understand the LTV and DTI regulations for your target area — they fundamentally determine how much you can borrow. Build the required deposit, which can be 30-80% in speculative zones. Consider starting with a jeonse rental to accumulate savings before buying

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

The Bogeumjari Loan through HF provides long-term fixed rates for owner-occupiers. The Didimdol Loan offers subsidised rates for first-time buyers. Youth-specific programmes provide reduced rates for under 34-39. Special programmes for newlywed couples (신혼부부 특례) provide enhanced terms

Check our [government grants guide](/south-korea/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In South Korea, the process works as follows.

Check government-backed loan eligibility (Bogeumjari, Didimdol) through the HF website first. For bank loans, compare rates using Banksalad (뱅크샐러드) or Finda (핀다). Understand which zone your target property falls in — speculative, regulated, or non-regulated

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not understanding zone-based LTV/DTI restrictions (buying in Gangnam requires a 60-80% deposit), not checking government-backed loan eligibility first (missing subsidised rates), not understanding DSR limits (all debts count, not just the mortgage), and not timing the purchase around government regulation changes

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/south-korea/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
