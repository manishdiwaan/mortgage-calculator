## Choosing the Right Mortgage in France

Selecting the right mortgage type is one of the most important financial decisions in the buying process. The wrong choice can cost you tens of thousands over the life of the loan. This guide explains every mortgage type available in France and helps you choose.

## Types of Mortgages Available

France is distinctive for offering long-term fully fixed rates. **Taux fixe (fixed rate)** locks your rate for 15, 20, or 25 years — the dominant product, chosen by the vast majority of French borrowers. This gives extraordinary payment certainty. **Taux variable (variable rate)** adjusts with the Euribor — less common in France (under 10% of market) and carries rate risk. **Taux variable capé (capped variable)** has a maximum rate ceiling (typically +1% or +2% above the initial rate) providing a safety net. All French mortgages are amortising — you repay principal and interest monthly. Interest-only is available only for investment purposes

## Head-to-Head Comparison

Fixed rate is the default choice in France — with 20-25 year fully fixed rates available at competitive levels, the certainty is hard to beat. Variable rates save money when Euribor is low but carry risk. Capped variable offers a compromise but the cap premium can offset savings. French rates are generally among the lowest in Europe for fixed-rate products, making the fixed-rate choice even more compelling

## Which Type Is Right for You?

Your ideal mortgage type depends on your risk tolerance, financial stability, and market outlook.

Choose **fixed rate** if you value payment certainty, are on a tight budget with little room for payment increases, or believe rates will rise. Choose **variable rate** if you can absorb payment increases, want the lowest possible initial cost, or believe rates will stay stable or fall.

Consider your loan term carefully. Shorter terms mean higher monthly payments but dramatically lower total interest. Our [mortgage guide](/france/mortgage-guide/) provides a detailed analysis of how term length affects total cost.

## Expert Tips for France

Use a courtier (broker) — they negotiate across multiple banks and often secure rates 0.1-0.3% below what you would get walking into a branch. The assurance emprunteur is a major hidden cost — use délégation d'assurance to get external insurance from day one rather than the bank's more expensive group contract. Under the Lemoine law, you can switch insurer at any time
