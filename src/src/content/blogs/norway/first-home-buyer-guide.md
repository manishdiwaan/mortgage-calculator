## Your First Home in Norway — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Norway is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Norway require a deposit of 15% minimum of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Save the required 15% kontantinsats (deposit) — this is strictly enforced. Reduce existing debts including studielån (student loans) as total debt cannot exceed 5x gross annual income. Consider BSU (boligsparing for ungdom) if still available — a tax-advantaged savings account for young buyers

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

First-time buyers are exempt from the 2.5% dokumentavgift (document duty). Husbanken provides startlån through municipalities for buyers who cannot qualify for conventional financing. These benefits can save kr 100,000-200,000+ on a typical purchase

Check our [government grants guide](/norway/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Norway, the process works as follows.

Get a finansieringsbevis (financing certificate) before house hunting — essential for participating in the budrunde (bidding process). Apply to your bank or DNB, Nordea, or SpareBank 1. Pre-approval takes 1-5 days for standard cases

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not getting finansieringsbevis before attending viewings (you cannot bid without it), not understanding the 5x income debt cap (student loans count toward this limit), overpaying in the budrunde (bidding process) due to emotional competition, and not taking advantage of the first-time buyer dokumentavgift exemption

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/norway/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
