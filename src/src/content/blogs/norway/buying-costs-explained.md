## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Norway?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Norway. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Dokumentavgift (document duty) of 2.5% of market value — exempt for first-time buyers. Tinglysingsgebyr (registration fees) of kr 500-600 for property and mortgage. Legal costs are minimal as the megler (estate agent) handles most of the process. Tilstandsrapport (condition report) is provided by the seller. Ejerskifteforsikring (change-of-ownership insurance) if desired. Total for first-time buyers: 1-2% (very low due to dokumentavgift exemption), non-first-time buyers: 3-5%

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

The 5x income debt cap means student loans directly reduce your borrowing capacity — every kr 100,000 of existing debt reduces your mortgage capacity by the same amount. Fellesutgifter (common expenses) for borettslag (cooperative housing) of kr 3,000-8,000/month cover heating, maintenance, and the cooperative's shared debt. Kommunale avgifter (municipal fees) for water, sewage, and waste are ongoing

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/norway/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
