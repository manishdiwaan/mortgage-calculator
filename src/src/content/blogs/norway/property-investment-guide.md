## Property Investment Guide — Norway 2026

Property investment can build long-term wealth through rental income and capital appreciation. However, it requires careful analysis of yields, taxes, financing costs, and market dynamics. This guide covers everything you need to know about investing in property in Norway.

## Rental Yields and Market Overview

Norwegian rental yields average 3-5%. Rental income is taxed at 22% (capital income rate). Expenses including mortgage interest, maintenance, insurance, and depreciation are deductible. Capital gains on investment property are taxed at 22%. Formuesskatt (wealth tax) applies to property values — currently assessed at 75% of market value for primary residence, 100% for secondary properties. ## Financing Investment Property

Investment property financing differs from owner-occupier mortgages. Lenders typically require higher deposits (20-40%), charge higher interest rates (0.5-2% premium), and apply stricter affordability criteria. Understanding the full tax picture is critical for investment returns. Investment due diligence goes beyond a standard purchase. Research local rental demand, vacancy rates, tenant quality, maintenance requirements, and comparable rental values.
