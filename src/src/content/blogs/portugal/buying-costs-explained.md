## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Portugal?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Portugal. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

IMT (transfer tax) on a sliding scale: 0% up to €101,917 for primary residences, increasing to 8% for properties above €1,050,400. Imposto de Selo (stamp duty) of 0.8% on the purchase price plus 0.6% on the loan amount. Notary and registry fees of €500-1,500. Comissão de avaliação (valuation) of €200-400. Comissão de dossier (processing fee) of €200-500. Mandatory insurance (life and property). Total: 6-10%

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

Vinculações (linked products like life insurance, home insurance, credit cards, and payroll domiciliation) reduce your spread but commit you to potentially expensive bank products. If you cancel them, your spread increases. IMI (annual property tax) of 0.3-0.8% of the taxable value is ongoing. Condominium fees of €50-200/month for apartments

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/portugal/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Portugal.

First-time buyers under 35 receive IMT exemption on properties up to a value threshold. Check our [government grants guide](/portugal/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=pt) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
