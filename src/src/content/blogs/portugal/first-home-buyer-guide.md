## Your First Home in Portugal — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Portugal is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Portugal require a deposit of 10-30% of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Build at least 10% deposit plus funds for IMT and notary costs (6-10% total). Open a savings plan at a Portuguese bank to demonstrate financial discipline. Some banks offer special rates for customers who domicile their salary (domiciliação de vencimento)

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

First-time buyers under 35 receive IMT exemption on properties up to a value threshold. Bonificação de juros (interest subsidies) have been introduced during high-rate periods. Some municipalities offer reduced IMI (property tax) for initial years. The government has focused on affordable housing through IHRU programmes

Check our [government grants guide](/portugal/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Portugal, the process works as follows.

Use ComparaJá.pt or Doutor Finanças to compare rates across all lenders. Get a pré-aprovação from at least 2-3 banks. Compare both the rate and the conditions tied to vinculações (linked products like insurance and payroll domiciliation)

Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=pt) to model different scenarios before applying. Read our [mortgage types guide](/portugal/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) to understand which loan product suits your situation.

## Understanding the Full Cost of Buying

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

IMT (transfer tax) on a sliding scale: 0% up to €101,917 for primary residences, increasing to 8% for properties above €1,050,400. Read our [buying costs guide](/portugal/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a complete breakdown of every expense.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not comparing rates across multiple banks (Portuguese rate spreads vary significantly), signing up for expensive vinculações (linked products) just to get a lower rate without checking if they offer good value, choosing variable rate without understanding Euribor risk, and not negotiating the spread aggressively

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/portugal/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.

The typical first home purchase follows these stages: establish your budget and savings target, explore government support options, get pre-approved for financing, search for the right property, make an offer, complete legal due diligence and inspections, and finalise settlement.
