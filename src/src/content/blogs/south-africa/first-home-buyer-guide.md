## Your First Home in South Africa — Where to Start

Buying your first home in South Africa is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in South Africa require a deposit of 0% (100% bonds available) of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

While 100% financing is available, having some savings for transfer costs is essential. Focus on maintaining a clean credit record — this is the single most important factor for bond approval and pricing. Clear any judgements, defaults, or arrears on your ITC record

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

FLISP provides a subsidy of R30,001-R130,505 for first-time buyers earning R3,501-R22,000/month. Transfer duty exemption on properties below R1,100,000. South Africa is one of the few markets globally where 100% financing is genuinely available, meaning zero deposit is needed for qualified buyers

Check our [government grants guide](/south-africa/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for the full list of current programmes and eligibility requirements.

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In South Africa, the process works as follows.

Use a bond originator (BetterBond or ooba) — their service is free and they submit to multiple banks simultaneously. Pre-qualification takes minutes and gives you a clear budget. Apply to all four major banks through the originator for the most competitive rate

Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=za) to model different scenarios before applying. Read our [mortgage types guide](/south-africa/blog/mortgage-types-compared/) to understand which loan product suits your situation.

## Understanding the Full Cost of Buying

The purchase price is just the beginning. Budget for additional costs that can add significantly to your total outlay.

Transfer duty: 0% up to R1,100,000, then 3-13% on a sliding scale above that. Read our [buying costs guide](/south-africa/blog/buying-costs-explained/) for a complete breakdown of every expense.

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not using a bond originator (missing competitive offers from multiple banks), having a poor credit record without addressing it first (even a cellphone contract in arrears impacts your rate), not understanding that transfer costs must come from savings even with 100% financing, and accepting the bank's first rate offer without negotiating

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/south-africa/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
