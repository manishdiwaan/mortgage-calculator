## Your First Home in Sweden — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Sweden is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Sweden require a deposit of 15% minimum of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Build the required 15% kontantinsats — this is strictly enforced across all Swedish banks. Reduce existing debts to improve your debt-to-income ratio. If buying a bostadsrätt (cooperative), also budget for the avgift (monthly fee) which affects affordability assessments

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

Sweden has limited direct buyer subsidies. ROT-avdrag (renovation tax deduction) provides a 30% deduction on labour costs for renovations. The amorteringskrav (mandatory amortisation) rules shape the landscape — 1% above 50% LTV, 2% above 70% LTV, and an additional 1% if the loan exceeds 4.5x gross income

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Sweden, the process works as follows.

Get a lånelöfte (loan promise) from your bank before house hunting — essential for bidding. Compare rates at SBAB (often the cheapest), Swedbank, SEB, Handelsbanken, and Nordea. Use Compricer.se for rate comparisons

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Not understanding the amorteringskrav (mandatory amortisation rules) which force significant monthly principal repayment, not investigating the bostadsrättsförening's (cooperative's) finances before buying, overpaying in the budgivning (bidding process), and not negotiating the rate — Swedish banks expect it

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/sweden/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
