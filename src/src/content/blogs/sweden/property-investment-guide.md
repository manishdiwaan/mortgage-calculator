## Property Investment Guide — Sweden 2026

Property investment can build long-term wealth through rental income and capital appreciation. However, it requires careful analysis of yields, taxes, financing costs, and market dynamics. This guide covers everything you need to know about investing in property in Sweden.

## Rental Yields and Market Overview

Swedish rental yields average 2-4% for apartments, higher for houses in some regions. Rental income from a subletting a bostadsrätt requires förening (cooperative) approval and rent is regulated. Capital gains tax of 22% on profit from property sale. ROT-avdrag on renovation can improve property value tax-efficiently. Uthyrning av privatbostad (private rental) allows tax-free rental income up to kr 40,000/year for primary residence subletting, with 20% tax above that

## Financing Investment Property

Investment property financing differs from owner-occupier mortgages. Lenders typically require higher deposits (20-40%), charge higher interest rates (0.5-2% premium), and apply stricter affordability criteria. Rental income may be partially factored into the assessment, but expect conservative treatment. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=se) to model investment scenarios.

## Tax Implications

Understanding the full tax picture is critical for investment returns. Rental income taxation, capital gains treatment, deductible expenses, and stamp duty or transfer tax surcharges on additional properties all affect your net return. ## Due Diligence for Investment

Investment due diligence goes beyond a standard purchase. Research local rental demand, vacancy rates, tenant quality, maintenance requirements, and comparable rental values.
