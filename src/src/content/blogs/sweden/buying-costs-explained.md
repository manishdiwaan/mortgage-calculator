## What Does It Really Cost to Buy in Sweden?

The purchase price is just part of the total cost of buying property in Sweden. Transaction taxes, legal fees, insurance, and other expenses can add significantly to your budget. Understanding every cost upfront prevents nasty surprises at settlement.

## Transaction Taxes and Government Charges

Lagfart (title registration) of 1.5% plus a fixed fee for houses (not applicable to bostadsrätter). Pantbrev (mortgage deed) of 2% — but only on new pantbrev amounts, as existing ones transfer free. No stamp duty. Agent fees paid by seller. Bostadsrätt purchases have no lagfart or pantbrev costs. Total: 0-3.5% depending on property type

## Hidden Costs Many Buyers Miss

Beyond the headline costs, several expenses catch first-time buyers off guard.

For bostadsrätt purchases, the monthly avgift (cooperative fee) of kr 3,000-8,000/month is a major ongoing cost covering heating, maintenance, and the cooperative's shared debt. The amorteringskrav forces 1-3% annual repayment which significantly increases effective monthly costs. UC (credit bureau) negative marks can persist for years. Existing pantbrev transfer free — always check how much pantbrev is already registered to the property

Always budget a contingency of 1-2% above your calculated costs to cover unexpected expenses. Our [first home buyer guide](/sweden/blog/first-home-buyer-guide/) covers budgeting strategies in detail.

## How to Reduce Your Buying Costs

Several strategies can reduce your total costs in Sweden.

Sweden has limited direct buyer subsidies. Check our [government grants guide](/sweden/blog/government-grants-schemes/) for current incentives.

Comparing mortgage offers across multiple lenders can also save you thousands over the life of the loan. Even a 0.25% rate difference compounds significantly over 20-30 years. Use our [mortgage calculator](/calculator/?country=se) to model different rate scenarios.

## Ongoing Costs of Ownership

Beyond the one-off purchase costs, property ownership involves ongoing expenses that you need to factor into your budget. These include property taxes, insurance, maintenance, and any applicable body corporate or management fees.

Budget approximately 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance and repairs, plus any local government charges and insurance premiums.

## Cost Comparison — New Build vs Resale

New build and resale properties often have different tax treatments. In many markets, new builds may attract VAT or GST instead of transfer tax, while resale properties may have lower tax rates based on assessed values. The total cost difference can be significant — always calculate both scenarios before deciding.
