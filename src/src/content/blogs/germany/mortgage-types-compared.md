## Choosing the Right Mortgage in Germany

Selecting the right mortgage type is one of the most important financial decisions in the buying process. The wrong choice can cost you tens of thousands over the life of the loan. This guide explains every mortgage type available in Germany and helps you choose.

## Types of Mortgages Available

Germany's Zinsbindung system is unlike most countries. **Annuitätendarlehen (annuity loan)** is the standard product — fixed monthly payments with a Zinsbindung (fixed-rate period) of 5, 10, 15, or 20 years. When the Zinsbindung expires, you refinance (Anschlussfinanzierung) at current rates. **Volltilger (full repayment loan)** repays entirely within the fixed period — higher payments but no refinancing risk. **KfW-Darlehen** are subsidised government loans for energy-efficient properties at below-market rates. **Bauspardarlehen** combines a savings phase with a guaranteed future loan rate. Under §489 BGB, you can exit any fixed-rate loan after 10 years with 6 months' notice, regardless of the original Zinsbindung

## Head-to-Head Comparison

A 10-year Zinsbindung is the most popular choice — balancing rate security with cost. Longer periods (15-20 years) cost 0.3-0.5% more per year but eliminate refinancing risk for longer. A Volltilger (full repayment within the fixed period) gets a rate discount but requires higher monthly payments. The §489 BGB right to exit after 10 years is a powerful safety valve for borrowers who chose a long Zinsbindung

## Which Type Is Right for You?

Your ideal mortgage type depends on your risk tolerance, financial stability, and market outlook.

Choose **fixed rate** if you value payment certainty, are on a tight budget with little room for payment increases, or believe rates will rise. Choose **variable rate** if you can absorb payment increases, want the lowest possible initial cost, or believe rates will stay stable or fall.

Consider your loan term carefully. Shorter terms mean higher monthly payments but dramatically lower total interest. Our [mortgage guide](/germany/mortgage-guide/) provides a detailed analysis of how term length affects total cost.
