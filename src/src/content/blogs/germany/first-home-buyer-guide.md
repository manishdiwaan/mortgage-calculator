## Your First Home in Germany — Where to Start

Buying your first home in Germany is one of the biggest financial decisions you will make. The process can feel overwhelming, but breaking it down into clear steps makes it manageable. This guide covers everything from saving your deposit to collecting the keys.

## Saving for Your Deposit

Most lenders in Germany require a deposit of 20-30% of the property price. The more you save, the better your loan terms and the less you pay in additional costs.

Build a substantial deposit of 20-30% plus enough for Kaufnebenkosten (transaction costs of 10-15%). Consider a Bausparvertrag (building savings contract) — a traditional German product combining savings with a future loan at a pre-agreed rate. Maintain a clean SCHUFA record, as any negative entries significantly impact mortgage approval

## Government Support for First-Time Buyers

KfW Bank offers subsidised loans for energy-efficient new builds through KfW 261, with below-market rates and repayment bonuses. Some Bundesländer (federal states) have their own programmes — Bavaria's BayernLabo and NRW's NRW.BANK offer targeted support. Wohn-Riester allows using state-subsidised retirement savings toward mortgage repayments. Direct buyer subsidies are limited at the federal level since the end of Baukindergeld

## Getting Pre-Approved for a Mortgage

Pre-approval tells you exactly how much you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer. In Germany, the process works as follows.

Use a mortgage broker (Vermittler) like Interhyp or Dr. Klein — they compare over 400 lenders for free. Get a Finanzierungszusage (financing commitment) before starting your property search. Ensure your SCHUFA score is checked and clean before applying

## Common First-Time Buyer Mistakes

Underestimating Kaufnebenkosten (10-15% of purchase price that must come from savings), not checking your SCHUFA score early (negative entries take years to clear), choosing too short a Zinsbindung (fixed-rate period) to save money without understanding the refinancing risk, and not shopping around with a Vermittler (broker) — individual bank offers vary significantly

Avoid these pitfalls by taking your time, doing thorough research, and leaning on qualified professionals throughout the process. Our [property inspection checklist](/germany/blog/property-inspection-checklist/) covers what to check before committing.
